\title{\textbf{When Does Generative Augmentation Help Extreme Coastal
Water-Level Forecasting?\\ Architecture Dependence, Physical Priors, and
Independent-Event Validation}}

\author{Yingda Yu \and Tong Mengxue \and Rong Jia}
\date{\today\\\textit{Preprint --- results consolidated; retrospective test split (see \S\ref{sec:discussion})}}

\begin{abstract}
Generative augmentation is widely proposed as the obvious remedy for the
scarcity of extreme events in coastal water-level training data, yet published
evaluations rarely control for event-level dependence, cross-architecture
pairing or generator fidelity. We report a pre-specified, five-region
evaluation programme (New York Bight, US Gulf Coast, Charleston, Southeast
Australia, Thames/North Sea; 2015--2024) that asks \emph{when} conditional
generative augmentation helps extreme non-tidal water-level forecasting under
a frozen 96\,h$\rightarrow$72\,h contract, and what the answer depends on.
The independent-event census is 679 union units (634 peak-over-threshold
clusters, 73 tropical cyclones, 28 matched), not the 707 a naive sum yields;
all inference is paired per event unit, never per sliding window. Under a
fixed-budget fairness contract (ten epochs, zero hyperparameter search) we
benchmark seven conditional generator configurations (including two Flow
Matching variants), verify every checkpoint
against its declared architecture, and find that generation quality spans
reproduced dispersion to numerical collapse: Flow Matching reproduces the
target dispersion (scale ratio near unity), while several families fail
 outright, and the failures are traceable to implementation defects
(terminal-step amplification, posterior collapse, unstable autoregressive
memory) rather than to model families. Fidelity, however, does not translate
into forecasting utility. In the primary analysis (20 pre-specified
augmentation-versus-baseline comparisons, Bonferroni $\alpha=0.0025$,
retrospective 2022--2024 test), 12 comparisons survive, splitting exactly 6
beneficial / 6 harmful: all four augmentation cells improve in Southeast
Australia ($-1.29$ to $-3.02\%$), all four harm in Thames/North Sea
($+3.44$ to $+4.31\%$), TCN cells benefit on the US Gulf Coast, TCN cells are
harmed in Charleston, and New York Bight is null-to-mixed. A paired
TCN-versus-GRU interaction test shows the popular ``TCN responds more''
pattern is region-dependent: only 4 of 10 comparisons survive correction, all
in beneficial regions, and a regional-heterogeneity test confirms region
identity moderates the effect (Cochran--Q $p \le 1.4\times10^{-10}$), with
effects attenuating at high event severity. A six-arm physical-prior ablation
(20 contrasts) yields only three surviving contrasts --- all Charleston GRU
cells, all indicating \emph{increased} error --- and we explicitly
distinguish ERA5 meteorological conditioning and physics-inspired statistical
regularisation from governing-equation constraints, which our method does not
implement. The results reframe generative augmentation for coastal forecasting
as a region- and architecture-dependent intervention whose benefits are
moderated by event severity, and whose global distributional fidelity is
neither necessary nor sufficient for extreme-event utility.
\end{abstract}
