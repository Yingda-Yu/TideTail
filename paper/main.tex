\title{\textbf{When Does Generative Augmentation Help Extreme Coastal
Water-Level Forecasting?\\ Architecture Dependence, Physical Priors, and
Independent-Event Validation}}

\author{Yingda Yu \and Tong Mengxue \and Rong Jia}
\date{\today\\\textit{Preprint --- evidence-linked; hypotheses not yet confirmed}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Extreme coastal water levels are rare, and generative augmentation is the
obvious way to make them less rare in training data. We ask when it actually
helps, and report a programme of controlled experiments in which the honest
answer is: not yet demonstrably, and for reasons that can be stated precisely.
Across five regions under a frozen 96\,h$\rightarrow$72\,h contract (2015--2024),
we first reconstruct the independent-event census from raw cluster artefacts.
The 2015--2024 union is \textbf{679} event units (634 peak-over-threshold
clusters, 73 tropical cyclones, 28 matched), not the 707 previously reported;
the 2024 test split contains 59 independent units, with no duplicate timestamps
and no cross-split window leakage. We then benchmark four conditional
generators on identical splits. Only Flow Matching reproduces the target
dispersion (generated/real scale ratio 0.945--1.116, Wasserstein-1 0.047);
conditional VAE and TimeGrad are under-dispersed by factors of 2.3 and 12,
and Diffusion-TS is rejected in all ten runs. An oracle-denoiser test proves
the reverse diffusion recursion itself correct (max absolute reconstruction
error $1.1\times10^{-10}$), which localises the failures to implementation
defects rather than model families; we report that two of the three proposed
repairs did \emph{not} work. Finally, after correcting cross-architecture
pairing, a $5{,}000\times$ p-value inflation and a Monte-Carlo resolution floor
that made significance arithmetically unreachable, the corrected event-level
analysis finds \textbf{zero seed-robust augmentation findings} across 320
comparisons. Effects are architecture-dependent (the TCN responds roughly forty
times more strongly than the GRU) and region-dependent (Thames is harmed for
both), and baseline seed variance alone reaches 25\%. We release the verified
event census, the fidelity benchmark, the corrected statistics and a three-year
split protocol built from scratch, and we state what would be required to
establish a positive claim.
\end{abstract}
