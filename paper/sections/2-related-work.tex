\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Coastal water-level and storm-surge forecasting}
Operational coastal water-level forecasting has long combined deterministic
tidal constituents with dynamical surge models; statistical and, more
recently, machine-learning components are added on top of these baselines.
\citet{bajo2010storm} combined a finite-element surge model with an artificial
neural network for the Venice lagoon, an architecture that remains
representative of operational hybrid chains. Data-driven deep learning for
surge prediction has since been explored at scale: \citet{tiggeloven2021exploring}
trained global neural networks on gauge and reanalysis data and found
competitive surge skill in coastal areas, and \citet{bruneau2020estimation}
estimated coastal sea-level extremes globally with neural networks.
Process understanding matters as much as model class: tide--surge interaction
structures the residual itself, so that in the North Sea the largest skew
surges occur preferentially near low water \citep{horsburgh2007tidesurge}.
At the operational end, pan-European extreme sea-level forecasts from the
Copernicus Marine Service show skill that is real but uneven across regions
and return periods \citep{irazoqui2022towards}. Two lessons carry over to this
work: the non-tidal residual is the operationally critical signal during
storms, and regional heterogeneity is the norm rather than the exception.

\subsection{Extreme-value statistics for coastal extremes}
The statistical backbone of extreme-event definition is peaks-over-threshold
modelling of exceedances over a high threshold \citep{davison1990models},
consolidated in the classical monograph of \citet{coles2001introduction}.
Because storm residuals cluster in time, declustering is required before
threshold exceedances can be treated as independent; the runs method and its
inference were formalised by \citet{ferro2003inference}. \citet{katz2002statistics}
review extremes in hydrology, emphasising that return-level estimates are only
as trustworthy as the independence assumptions behind them. Our event census
(POT clusters with 168\,h declustering, unioned with canonical tropical
cyclones under a frozen one-to-one matching rule) is a direct application of
this tradition, and the double-counting failure documented in
Section~\ref{sec:results-census} is precisely the kind of error the
declustering literature warns about.

\subsection{Data augmentation for time series}
Augmentation for time series is surveyed by \citet{wen2021time}, with
empirical evidence for classification tasks in \citet{iwana2021empirical}.
Classical stochastic methods decompose and resample the series: bagging with
STL decomposition and Box--Cox transformation \citep{bergmeir2016bagging}, and
window-warping or magnitude-warping style perturbations validated on wearable
sensor data \citep{um2017data}. Generative augmentation replaces synthetic
perturbation with learned conditional sampling, raising a question these
surveys do not answer for our setting: does a generatively ``better'' sample
distribution improve downstream forecasting of extremes, or only the bulk?

\subsection{Conditional GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models for time series}
VAEs learn latent variable models through the variational bound of
\citet{kingma2014autoencoding}; GP-VAE extends them to probabilistic time
series imputation \citep{fortuin2020gpvae}. On the adversarial side,
\citet{esteban2017realvalued} introduced recurrent conditional GANs for
real-valued medical series, \citet{yoon2019timegan} proposed TimeGAN with
stepwise supervised loss, and \citet{xu2020cotgan} addressed sequential data
through causal optimal transport. Diffusion models, introduced for images by
\citet{ho2020denoising}, now dominate time-series generation:
CSDI for probabilistic imputation \citep{tashiro2021csdi}, TimeGrad for
autoregressive probabilistic forecasting \citep{rasul2021timegrad},
Diffusion-TS with interpretable decomposition \citep{yuan2024diffusionts},
and structured state-space variants for imputation and forecasting
\citep{alcaraz2023diffusion}. Flow Matching \citep{lipman2023flow} offers a
simulation-free alternative built on continuous normalising flows, later
generalised and connected to minibatch optimal transport
\citep{tong2024improving} and unified with diffusion through stochastic
interpolants \citep{albergo2025stochastic}; Neural Flows provide efficient
continuous-time alternatives \citep{bilos2021neural}. Training failures and
evaluation pitfalls of GANs are well documented: the Wasserstein reformulation
\citep{arjovsky2017wasserstein} and its principled-analysis companion
\citep{arjovsky2017towards}, gradient-penalty training
\citep{gulrajani2017wgangp}, and precision/recall or FID-style diagnostics for
generative quality \citep{sajjadi2018assessing,heusel2017gans}. Our
seven-configuration benchmark inherits both the model families and the evaluation
cautions from this literature.

\subsection{Physics-guided machine learning for Earth systems}
\citet{reichstein2019deep} argued that the next step for Earth-system science
is hybrid modelling that couples deep learning with process understanding.
Concrete instantiations include physics-guided RNNs for lake temperatures
\citep{jia2019physics} and the broader taxonomy of physics-informed machine
learning collected by \citet{karniadakis2021physics}, with engineering and
environmental applications surveyed by \citet{willard2022integrating}. We
adopt their terminology deliberately and conservatively: our ``physical
priors'' are ERA5 meteorological conditioning and physics-inspired
statistical regularisation, not governing-equation PDE constraints. Nothing
in our six-arm ablation should be read as constraining shallow-water
dynamics.

\subsection{Event-level forecast evaluation}
Verification theory distinguishes distributional skill from event skill:
strictly proper scoring rules \citep{gneiting2007strictly}, scale-selective
verification for high-impact convective events \citep{roberts2008scale}, the
extreme dependency score for rare events \citep{stephenson2008extreme}, and
forecast verification tailored to extreme-value distributions
\citep{friederichs2012forecast}. Our evaluation protocol follows the same
principle: paired, event-unit-level sign-flip tests with hierarchy-corrected
multiple-testing control, stratified by background/moderate/extreme intensity,
because bulk distributional fidelity is exactly the quantity that our results
show does not automatically transfer to extreme-event utility.

\subsection{Positioning of this work}
Prior applications of generative augmentation to geophysical forecasting
typically report aggregate skill on held-out windows. What is missing, and
what this paper contributes, is a controlled account of \emph{when}
generative augmentation helps extreme coastal forecasting at all: one
protocol, five contrasting regions, two downstream architectures, seven
generator families under one fidelity gate and one matched budget, a
six-arm physical-prior ablation, and inference keyed to independent event
units rather than overlapping windows. The niche we claim is therefore not
``first diffusion augmentation for tides'' but the first systematic
region- and architecture-resolved audit of generative augmentation for
extreme coastal water-level forecasting, with the fidelity-versus-utility
gap as its central empirical finding.
