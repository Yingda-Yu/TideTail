\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked when generative augmentation helps extreme coastal water-level
forecasting. The answer assembled here is not ``yes'' or ``no'' but a
measured map of dependence: the effect is region- and architecture-dependent,
moderated by event severity, and poorly predicted by the generator's global
distributional fidelity.

\textbf{What is established.} Under one frozen protocol
(2015--2020/2021/2022--2024, retrospective test) and one audited inference
machinery: 12 of 20 pre-specified augmentation-versus-baseline comparisons
survive Bonferroni correction, splitting exactly 6 beneficial / 6 harmful.
All four augmentation cells improve in Southeast Australia ($-1.29$ to
$-3.02\%$); all four harm in Thames/North Sea ($+3.44$ to $+4.31\%$); TCN
cells improve on the US Gulf Coast and are harmed in Charleston; New York
Bight is null-to-mixed. Regional heterogeneity is a tested finding, not a
description (Cochran--Q $p \le 1.4\times10^{-10}$ on every arm), and the
paired TCN-versus-GRU interaction survives correction in only 3 of 10
comparisons --- all in beneficial regions --- so ``TCN responds more'' is
descriptive, not a law. Effects concentrate in low-to-moderate severity
events and attenuate at high severity in every region with a severity
gradient, which is the opposite of what tail-motivated augmentation is asked
to deliver.

\textbf{What the generator benchmark adds.} Seven families under one
fairness contract (fixed budget, zero search, verified checkpoints,
season-stratified scoring) separate cleanly into reproduced dispersion
(Flow Matching), informative failures with line-level diagnoses
(posterior collapse, unstable autoregressive memory, terminal-step
amplification), and outright numerical collapse. Fidelity does not translate
into utility: the downstream result was carried by a generator whose own
fidelity status is inconclusive, and the pilot's fidelity-rejected
generators produced only harms. Global distributional quality is neither
necessary nor sufficient for extreme-event forecasting value.

\textbf{What the physical priors add.} The six-arm ablation is bounded and
negative: no arm beats standard Flow Matching consistently, the only
surviving contrasts (three Charleston GRU regulariser contrasts) indicate
\emph{increased} error, and ERA5 conditioning alone is not reliably
beneficial. The method tested is meteorological conditioning plus
physics-inspired statistical regularisation --- explicitly not a
governing-equation, PDE-constrained generator --- and claims of that kind
require different machinery entirely.

\textbf{What is preserved as negative.} The repaired VAE samples worse than
its legacy version; the repaired Diffusion-TS diverges to NaN once it fits
the data; the historical candidate-zoo analysis yields zero seed-robust
findings; the pilot's surviving downstream effects are all harms of
fidelity-rejected generators; and no seasonal moderation is detected.
Reporting these plainly is part of the contribution: the alternative ---
re-tuning until the number moves --- is precisely how this literature
acquires effects that do not replicate.

\textbf{What would strengthen the answer.} An untouched confirmatory test
period beyond 2024, with 30--40 independent units per region; more generator
seeds so seed spread enters the uncertainty budget rather than being
averaged away; tail-targeted generator variants whose validation gains
survive a frozen retrospective evaluation (the bounded study of
Section~\ref{sec:results-tail-calibration}); a receptive-field ablation to
test the architecture-interaction account; and a physics arm that encodes
governing equations rather than statistical regularisation. The
infrastructure for all of these --- the frozen protocol, the verified census,
the stratified fidelity benchmark, the audited paired statistics and the
declared testing families --- now exists in this repository, and every
number in this paper traces to an immutable artifact.
