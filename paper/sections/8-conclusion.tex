\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked when generative augmentation helps extreme coastal water-level
forecasting. The evidence assembled here does not answer ``yes'' or ``no''; it
identifies the conditions under which the question can be answered at all, and
shows that several previously reported answers were artefacts of the
measurement.

Three results are established. \textbf{First}, the independent-event census for
five regions over 2015--2024 is 679 units, not 707: 634 POT clusters and 73
tropical cyclones overlap in 28 peaks, and the 2024 test split contains 59
window-eligible extreme units with no duplicate timestamps or cross-split
leakage. \textbf{Second}, of four conditional generators trained under one
contract, only Flow Matching reproduces the target dispersion (scale ratio
0.945--1.116, Wasserstein-1 0.047); the VAE and TimeGrad are under-dispersed by
factors of 2.3 and 12 and Diffusion-TS is rejected in all ten runs. An
oracle-denoiser test proves the reverse diffusion recursion correct to
$1.1\times10^{-10}$, so these are implementation failures, not family verdicts.
A seven-family fixed-budget pilot under one matched contract (ten epochs, zero
hyperparameter search for every family) is consistent with this picture: only
the two Flow Matching variants and WGAN-GP avoid fidelity rejection, and the
eleven Bonferroni-surviving downstream effects are all \emph{harms} of
fidelity-rejected generators — confirmations of broken implementations, not
rankings of families.
\textbf{Third}, under corrected event-level inference --- within-architecture
pairing, a repaired bootstrap p-value, and a Monte-Carlo resolution floor below
the correction threshold --- no augmentation effect survives a 320-test
Bonferroni family, and baseline seed variance alone reaches 25\%.

Two results are negative about our own proposed fixes and are preserved as such:
the repaired VAE samples worse than the legacy version, and the repaired
Diffusion-TS diverges to NaN once it actually fits the data. Reporting these as
failures is a substantive part of the contribution, because the alternative ---
re-tuning until the number moves in the expected direction --- is precisely how
this literature acquires effects that do not replicate.

Three observations constrain the search for a positive result. The response is
\emph{architecture-dependent}: the TCN improves in 110 of 160 comparisons
(mean $-7.08\%$) against 86 of 160 for the GRU (mean $-0.17\%$). It is
\emph{region-dependent}: Thames is harmed for both architectures, consistent
with a macrotidal regime in which surge is a small fraction of total water
level. And it is \emph{fragile}: the three comparisons that survive their
within-region correction each hold against only one of two baseline seeds.

What would move H1 from untested to established is therefore specific, and none
of it is optional. An event-level design with at least 30--40 independent units
per region in the test window, which requires expanding the test period beyond
2022--2024. Baselines reported across multiple seeds, with the seed spread
entering the uncertainty budget rather than being averaged away. A multiplicity
family that enumerates the actual search, including tail-aware and discarded
configurations. A generator whose sampling path is verified against the reverse
recursion, with a learned or free-bits prior so that training and sampling share
one distribution. And, if physical priors are to be claimed, a causal
reanalysis-conditioned design with a downscaling step that closes the 0.25$^\circ$
representativeness gap.

The infrastructure needed to run that study now exists in this repository: the
frozen contract, the verified census, the fidelity gate, the corrected paired
statistics and a from-scratch three-year split protocol. The contribution of
this paper is to have built it, to have used it to remove several results that
did not survive it, and to have reported that plainly.
