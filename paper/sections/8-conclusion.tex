\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We investigated whether conditional diffusion augmentation can generate physically
plausible extreme coastal water-level residuals that improve downstream forecasting
on independent real storm events. The answer is \emph{not yet}: our pilot DDPM
produced samples whose standard deviation is approximately 23 times the training
target SD, with magnitudes exceeding $\pm 16$ metres. This failure is reproduced
on both CUDA and CPU, and traced to terminal-step amplification in the cosine
noise schedule combined with imperfect epsilon prediction at a limited training
budget.

The oracle schedule comparison (Table~\ref{tab:oracle-schedules}) further isolates
the terminal schedule shape as the principal driver: replacing a 64-step cosine
schedule whose terminal $\bar{\alpha}_{64} \approx 5.93 \times 10^{-7}$ by a
subsampled 1000-step schedule with $\bar{\alpha}_{64} \approx 5.89 \times 10^{-4}$
reduces sample $\sigma$ from 4.92~m to 0.297~m (1.38$\times$ the training reference).
This probe is not a scientific fix --- the checkpoint's epsilon predictions are
schedule-specific --- but it establishes that \emph{terminal schedule geometry}
is a structural cause of the explosion, not merely a symptom of insufficient
training budget.

More importantly, this work establishes \emph{what is needed} before diffusion
augmentation can be meaningfully evaluated for coastal water-level forecasting,
and implements it:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A frozen data protocol (FORECAST\_TASK\_CONTRACT v1) with 96h$\rightarrow$72h
    main task, train-only scalers/thresholds, and full-support embargo-safe event
    splitting. Five regions are admitted; Hong Kong is excluded pending signoff.
    \item Strong neural forecasting baselines on the formal contract: GRU encoder-decoder
    and TCN with matched parameter budgets, plus persistence and seasonal baselines.
    \item A balanced oversampler (A1) and physics-justified training-set augmentation
    (A2, residual noise + tidal phase shift).
    \item A controlled retraining of the diffusion model (A3/A4) with schedule
    investigation, followed by downstream forecasting evaluation on independent
    real storm events with paired bootstrap confidence intervals.
\end{enumerate}

The \texttt{trae/research-recovery-20261030} branch now contains 127 passing
unit tests, zero Ruff warnings, and 7 committed builds over the original
recovery merge. The frozen data protocol, embargo-safe splitting, training-set
augmentation, and evaluation infrastructure prevent reintroduction of known
leakage and generation-quality failures in future work. The claim in
\texttt{paper/CLAIMS\_LEDGER.md} remains exactly as it was: H1 and H2 are
\textbf{untested hypotheses}. The contribution of this paper is to record
honestly what we have found, what we have ruled out, what infrastructure now
exists, and what remains to be done.
