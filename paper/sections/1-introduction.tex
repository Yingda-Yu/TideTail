\section{Introduction}

Coastal flooding is among the most costly natural hazards, and the events that
matter most operationally are the ones that are rarest in the historical
record. A forecaster trained on ten years of gauge observations therefore spends
most of its capacity on benign conditions. Generative augmentation is the
obvious response: synthesise additional extreme residuals so that the
downstream forecaster sees more of the tail. The question this paper asks is
narrower and more useful than ``does augmentation work'': \emph{when} does
generative augmentation help extreme coastal water-level forecasting, and what
does the answer depend on?

We began with the standard hypothesis, stated here in its original form so that
the reader can see what the evidence did to it:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{H1}: Tail-aware conditional generative augmentation, applied under a
matched forecaster training budget, improves prediction accuracy on
\emph{independent held-out real storm events} relative to real-only training,
standard oversampling, perturbation augmentation, and standard conditional
diffusion.
\end{quote}

The evidence assembled here supports neither a blanket acceptance nor a
blanket rejection of H1. It supports a different, more structured answer:
augmentation effects on extreme-event error are \emph{region-dependent} and
\emph{architecture-dependent}, are moderated by event severity, and are
poorly predicted by the global distributional fidelity of the generator. The
contribution of this paper is less a verdict than a measured map of that
dependence, obtained under controls that published evaluations of time-series
augmentation frequently omit: event-level pairing, within-architecture
baselines, declared multiple-testing families, verified generator
checkpoints, and an explicit fairness contract for the generator comparison.

\subsection{Why the question is harder than it looks}

Four problems recur in published and internal evaluations of generative
augmentation for time series, and all four were present in our own earlier
work.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Window overlap masquerades as sample size.} A 96\,h$\rightarrow$72\,h
    contract produces tens of thousands of sliding windows per region, but a
    single storm supplies hundreds of them. Treating windows as independent
    inflates every confidence interval by roughly the square root of the
    within-event window count. The unit of inference must be the \emph{event},
    not the window.
    \item \textbf{Event counts are easy to double-count.} Tropical-cyclone
    catalogues and peak-over-threshold (POT) declustering describe overlapping
    phenomena. Adding the two totals without computing the intersection inflates
    the census; in our own repository this produced a decade count of 707
    instead of 679 (Section~\ref{sec:results-census}).
    \item \textbf{Generator failures are attributed to families rather than to
    code.} A diffusion model whose reverse sampler is correct but whose Fourier
    regulariser regresses the wrong target will fail, and it is tempting to
    conclude that diffusion is unsuited to the problem. It is not a
    family-level conclusion; it is a line-level defect
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-recovery}).
    \item \textbf{Augmentation effects are read globally.} Averaging a
    treatment effect over regions and architectures can return zero while the
    per-region effects are large, opposite in sign, and individually
    significant. Without an explicit heterogeneity test, a genuine regional
    signal is reported as a null result --- or, worse, a regional gain is
    promoted to a universal claim.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A verified event census and frozen event-level inference.} We
    recompute the 2015--2024 independent-event union for five regions from raw
    cluster artefacts (634 POT clusters, 73 tropical cyclones, 28 matched
    $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{679} units), check duplicate timestamps and
    cross-split leakage, and run every primary test as a per-event-unit paired
    comparison within region and architecture. The five regional event-unit
    sets (40--51 units each) are never pooled as independent storms.
    \item \textbf{A seven-configuration generative benchmark (two Flow Matching
    variants, CVAE, TimeGrad, DDPM, WGAN-GP and Diffusion-TS) under one fairness
    contract.} Standard and tail-aware Flow Matching, conditional VAE,
    TimeGrad, conditional DDPM, conditional WGAN-GP and Diffusion-TS are
    trained with fixed defaults (ten epochs, batch 256, zero hyperparameter
    search) on identical splits, with season-stratified fidelity scoring,
    background/moderate/extreme strata, and checkpoint verification
    (architecture, parameter counts, SHA256) for every cell of the
    5\,regions\,$\times$\,7\,families\,$\times$\,2\,generator-seed grid. Flow
    Matching reproduces the target dispersion; several families fail outright,
    for line-level reasons we diagnose explicitly.
    \item \textbf{A pre-specified five-region augmentation evaluation.} Twenty
    primary comparisons (5 regions $\times$ 4 augmentation arms) under one
    frozen protocol (2015--2020 train / 2021 validation / 2022--2024
    retrospective test): \textbf{12 of 20 survive Bonferroni correction at
    $\alpha = 0.0025$, splitting exactly 6 beneficial / 6 harmful}. All four
    augmentation cells improve in Southeast Australia ($-1.29$ to
    $-3.02\%$), all four harm in Thames/North Sea ($+3.44$ to $+4.31\%$), the
    TCN cells improve on the US Gulf Coast and are harmed in Charleston, and
    New York Bight is null-to-mixed.
    \item \textbf{A paired architecture-interaction test.} On matched event
    units, the TCN-versus-GRU difference in augmentation response survives
    correction in only 4 of 10 comparisons, all in beneficial regions. The
    popular ``TCN responds more'' pattern is therefore descriptive, not a
    universal architecture effect (Section~\ref{sec:results-expanded}).
    \item \textbf{Tested regional heterogeneity and severity dependence.} A
    Cochran--Q test rejects effect homogeneity across regions for every
    augmentation arm ($p \le 1.4\times10^{-10}$), pairwise Holm tests
    localise the differences, and per-event severity analysis shows effects
    attenuating at high severity: benefits concentrate in low-to-moderate
    events, and harmful regions are most harmful there too
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-heterogeneity}).
    \item \textbf{A six-arm physical-prior ablation with an explicitly bounded
    interpretation.} ERA5-conditioned, pressure-, wind-, combined- and
    tail-aware Flow Matching variants are compared against standard FM in two
    regions (20 primary contrasts). Only three Charleston GRU contrasts
    survive Bonferroni correction, all indicating \emph{increased} error. We
    state plainly what the method is --- meteorological conditioning plus
    physics-inspired statistical regularisation --- and what it is not: a
    governing-equation, PDE-constrained generator.
    \item \textbf{Corrected statistics, preserved negative results.} We fix
    (and document) a cross-architecture pairing error, a $\approx5{,}000\times$
    bootstrap p-value inflation, a Monte-Carlo resolution defect that could
    emit $p=0$, and a Bonferroni flag that compared raw rather than adjusted
    p-values. Repaired generator implementations that made sampling
    \emph{worse} are reported as failures rather than re-tuned away.
\end{enumerate}

We do not claim H1 is universally supported: it is supported where the
regions and architectures that benefit support it, and refuted where they do
not. We also do not call the 2022--2024 retrospective test an untouched
confirmatory holdout; the split was used during protocol development in
earlier waves, and every downstream claim carries that disclosure.

\subsection{Paper structure}

Section~\ref{sec:related} positions the work. Section~\ref{sec:protocol}
describes the frozen data protocol and the event census. Section~\ref{sec:method}
describes the seven generator configurations, the forecasters, and the physical-prior
variants. Section~\ref{sec:exp} states the experimental design, fairness
contract and multiple-testing families. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the
census, the generator benchmark and recovery study, the five-region primary
result, the architecture-interaction and heterogeneity analyses, the six-arm
ablation, and the preserved historical analyses. Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
interprets the mechanisms; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} states what would be
required to turn the regional map into an operational recommendation.
