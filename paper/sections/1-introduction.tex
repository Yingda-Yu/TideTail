\section{Introduction}

Coastal flooding is among the most costly natural hazards, and the events that
matter most operationally are the ones that are rarest in the historical
record. A forecaster trained on ten years of gauge observations therefore spends
most of its capacity on benign conditions. Generative augmentation is the
obvious response: synthesise additional extreme residuals so that the
downstream forecaster sees more of the tail. The question this paper asks is
narrower and more useful than ``does augmentation work'': \emph{when} does
generative augmentation help extreme coastal water-level forecasting, and what
does the answer depend on?

We began with the standard hypothesis, stated here in its original form so that
the reader can see what the evidence did to it:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{H1}: Tail-aware conditional generative augmentation, applied under a
matched forecaster training budget, improves prediction accuracy on
\emph{independent held-out real storm events} relative to real-only training,
standard oversampling, perturbation augmentation, and standard conditional
diffusion.
\end{quote}

H1 is still not established. What this paper contributes is the reason
it is not established, and the conditions under which a future study could
establish it.

\subsection{Why the question is harder than it looks}

Three problems recur in published and internal evaluations of generative
augmentation for time series, and all three were present in our own earlier
work.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Window overlap masquerades as sample size.} A 96\,h$\rightarrow$72\,h
    contract produces tens of thousands of sliding windows per region, but a
    single storm supplies hundreds of them. Treating windows as independent
    inflates every confidence interval by roughly the square root of the
    within-event window count. The unit of inference must be the \emph{event},
    not the window.
    \item \textbf{Event counts are easy to double-count.} Tropical-cyclone
    catalogues and peak-over-threshold (POT) declustering describe overlapping
    phenomena. Adding the two totals without computing the intersection inflates
    the census; in our own repository this produced a decade count of 707
    instead of 679 (Section~\ref{sec:results-census}).
    \item \textbf{Generator failures are attributed to families rather than to
    code.} A diffusion model whose reverse sampler is correct but whose Fourier
    regulariser regresses the wrong target will fail, and it is tempting to
    conclude that diffusion is unsuited to the problem. It is not a
    family-level conclusion; it is a line-level defect
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-recovery}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A verified event census.} We recompute the 2015--2024
    independent-event union for five regions from raw cluster artefacts
    (634 POT clusters, 73 tropical cyclones, 28 matched $\Rightarrow$
    \textbf{679} units), and show that the 2024 test split contains
    \textbf{59} independent units. Duplicate timestamps and cross-split
    forecast-window leakage are checked and found absent.
    \item \textbf{A four-generator benchmark on identical splits.} Conditional
    VAE, TimeGrad, Diffusion-TS and Flow Matching are compared under one
    contract with one fidelity gate. Flow Matching is the only generator that
    reproduces the target dispersion ($0.945$--$1.116$), and even it does not
    clear the full gate.
    \item \textbf{A diagnostic recovery study.} We prove the DDPM reverse
    recursion correct with an oracle denoiser (max absolute error
    $1.1\times10^{-10}$), localise three implementation defects, and report
    honestly that \emph{two of the three proposed repairs did not work} ---
    the repaired VAE samples \emph{worse}, and the repaired Diffusion-TS
    diverges to NaN. This is a negative result about our repairs, not about
    the families.
    \item \textbf{A corrected event-level evaluation.} After fixing cross-architecture
    pairing, a $5{,}000\times$ p-value inflation and a Monte-Carlo resolution
    floor above the correction threshold, we find \textbf{zero}
    seed-robust augmentation findings across 320 comparisons. Effects are
    architecture-dependent (TCN responds, GRU barely does) and region-dependent
    (Thames is harmed), and baseline seed variance alone reaches 25\%.
    \item \textbf{A physics-prior pilot and a three-year protocol.} Causal
    ERA5-conditioned Flow Matching variants are trained and evaluated
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-physics}), and a 2015--2020/2021/2022--2024
    protocol is implemented from scratch because no existing checkpoint can be
    evaluated on 2022--2024 (Section~\ref{sec:results-expanded}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Paper structure}

Section~\ref{sec:related} positions the work. Section~\ref{sec:protocol}
describes the frozen data protocol. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the
generators and the forecasters. Section~\ref{sec:exp} states the experimental
design. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the census, the generator benchmark,
the recovery study, the corrected event-level analysis, the physics pilot and
the expanded protocol. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} explains the mechanisms;
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} states what would be required to move H1 from
untested to established.
