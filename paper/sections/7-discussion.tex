\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Flow Matching produces plausible samples}
\label{subsec:why-fm}

Flow Matching is the only generator in our benchmark whose sample dispersion
matches the data (Table~\ref{tab:generators}). Three structural differences
explain it, and each is a mechanism rather than an empirical coincidence.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{One regression, one ODE.} The training objective is a single
    velocity-regression loss on straight-line interpolants between a Gaussian
    prior and the data. Inference integrates the learned field forward with a
    fixed-step Euler solver. There is no chain of 64 conditional updates whose
    errors compound, so the failure mode that dominates DDPM --- amplification of
    terminal-step epsilon error --- simply does not exist.
    \item \textbf{No small-variance sub-space.} Diffusion-TS first decomposes the
    signal into trend, seasonal and residual components and diffuses the
    residual, which concentrates the problem into a low-variance sub-space where
    a fixed absolute epsilon error is relatively enormous. Flow Matching diffuses
    nothing: it transports the full conditional residual distribution, so the
    signal-to-noise ratio of the regression target stays constant across
    training.
    \item \textbf{Conditioning enters as a feature, not as an additive bias.} The
    velocity network is a temporal convolutional network that consumes the
    history (and, in our physics variants, causal ERA5 summaries) directly, so
    conditioning cannot be lost through a rank deficiency such as the scalar
    latent broadcast that crippled the VAE.
\end{enumerate}

This does not make Flow Matching \emph{sufficient}: every FM run is formally
inconclusive because peak-tail agreement is not established at ten runs. It
makes it the only generator that is currently \emph{eligible} for a downstream
test.

\subsection{Why the other three implementations struggled}
\label{subsec:why-others}

None of the three failures is a property of its model family.

\paragraph{Conditional VAE: a sampling-path mismatch.}
The original defect (a scalar latent projection) was real and worth fixing, but
fixing it made sampling \emph{worse}. The reason is that the VAE samples from
$\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ while training fits a conditional posterior whose aggregate
has collapsed to a narrow distribution (0/32 active dimensions). The decoder is
therefore always evaluated slightly off-manifold, and a stronger
$z\mapsto$horizon path amplifies the discrepancy. The correct remedy is to make
training and sampling share a distribution --- free-bits KL, latent dropout, or a
learned conditional prior --- none of which we ran. Posterior collapse worsened
with training length (16/32 active dimensions at 4,000 windows versus 0/32 at
20,000), which is the standard dynamic and not a dataset property.

\paragraph{TimeGrad: an unstable autoregressive state.}
Consuming the ignored memory was necessary and helped (scale 10.55 $\rightarrow$
6.09), but the memory definition itself is unstable. A raw cumulative sum grows
monotonically across 72 horizon steps and was never seen during teacher-forced
training, so it diverges ($1.9\times10^{7}$ in the smaller pilot). A mean
aggregation removes the growth but also removes the signal, and the recursion
diverges in the opposite direction ($1.22\times10^{5}$). A bounded or decayed
memory is the obvious next hypothesis; we did not test it.

\paragraph{Diffusion-TS: numerical amplification in the objective.}
The original Fourier defect (noise spectrum versus clean spectrum) was real. The
repair replaced it with the implied clean signal, which requires dividing by
$\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$. At the terminal step this factor is $\approx0.024$, so
both the value and its gradient are amplified roughly $41\times$; the run reaches
NaN. The lesson is that an objective which is algebraically correct can still be
numerically unusable at a schedule's endpoint, and that a 2$\times$ improvement
observed at a small budget can reverse once the model actually fits the data.

\subsection{Why GRU and TCN respond differently}
\label{subsec:why-arch}

Under corrected within-architecture pairing, the TCN improves in 110 of 160
comparisons (mean $-7.08\%$) while the GRU improves in 86 of 160 (mean
$-0.17\%$; Figure~\ref{fig:arch}). This is a large relative difference and it
reverses the earlier, incorrectly paired conclusion that architecture effects
dwarf augmentation effects.

We can state the comparison and bound the explanation, but we cannot yet prove
the mechanism. The most plausible account is capacity allocation: the TCN's
dilated receptive field integrates the full 96\,h history through stacked
convolutions, and synthetic windows add high-frequency, storm-like variability
that the convolutional filters can exploit as data-driven regularisation. The
GRU's gating saturates on the same inputs, so extra samples shift the operating
point rather than the effective receptive field. An alternative account is that
the GRU is already closer to its achievable error floor on these splits, leaving
less head for augmentation to recover. Both accounts predict the observed
direction; discriminating between them requires a receptive-field ablation and a
learning-curve study that we did not run, and we therefore present them as
hypotheses rather than findings.

\subsection{Why some regions benefit and others do not}
\label{subsec:why-region}

Thames is the only region where augmentation harms both architectures
(+9.78\% GRU, +4.88\% TCN; Figure~\ref{fig:arch}). The physical reading is
straightforward: Thames is macrotidal (approximately 6.3~m range) and surge is a
small fraction of total water level, so the residual signal has low
signal-to-noise ratio. A generator fitted to that residual produces samples whose
statistical structure is dominated by noise, and appending them adds variance
without adding storm information. Thames also has 24.7\% NaN gaps in its source
series, which further weakens the fit.

Southeast Australia shows the largest TCN gain ($-21.69\%$) yet essentially no
GRU gain ($-0.59\%$), with a baseline MAE already near 0.05~m. A large
\emph{relative} change on a small absolute error is easy to obtain and should not
be read as a large operational benefit. NY Bight and the Gulf show moderate
gains for both architectures; Charleston, the region where the withdrawn
``significant'' claim originated, shows $-4.30\%$ for the GRU and $-0.09\%$ for
the TCN under correction.

A single cross-region correlation (tidal range versus gain, $N=5$) cannot
support a causal claim, and we do not make one.

\subsection{What independent-event evaluation changes}
\label{subsec:why-events}

Three things change, and all three move in the conservative direction.

First, \textbf{the effective sample size collapses}. A 2024 test split supplies
6--15 extreme units per region, not thousands of windows, so the standard error
of an extreme-window MAE is an order of magnitude larger than a window-level
analysis implies. Second, \textbf{multiplicity becomes binding}. A 320-test
family at $\alpha=1.563\times10^{-4}$ is not satisfied by any of the 320
comparisons once pairing is fixed. Third, \textbf{baseline variability becomes
comparable to the effect}. Identical A0 recipes differ by up to 25\% across seeds
(Figure~\ref{fig:seedvar}), which is the same order as the mean augmentation
effect in most regions. Any claim resting on a single baseline seed is therefore
unsupported, whether or not it clears a nominal p-value.

One honest limitation remains: the 320-test family does not cover the full search
space, because tail-aware predictions and discarded hyper-parameters were never
materialised. The correction is anti-conservative, which strengthens rather than
weakens the conclusion that nothing survives.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resolution.} ERA5 is 0.25$^\circ$; the Charleston anchor is
    11.7~km from its cell centre, so near-coastal pressure and wind are cell
    averages. No downscaling was attempted.
    \item \textbf{Causality.} Physics covariates are causal (history only), but
    the forcing is reanalysis. This is a reanalysis-conditioned experiment, not
    an operational forecast experiment.
    \item \textbf{Event units.} The expanded 2022--2024 units are POT-only, since
    the canonical-TC overlay is absent from the existing prediction archives.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint reuse.} No existing checkpoint can be evaluated on
    2022--2024; the expanded protocol required retraining from scratch.
    \item \textbf{Compute.} All recovery pilots are single-region CPU runs;
    GPU0 is prohibited and every other GPU was occupied. Conclusions are
    correspondingly scoped.
    \item \textbf{Thames data quality.} 24.7\% of the source timeline is NaN,
    which both reduces its unit count and weakens any generator fitted to it.
\end{itemize}
