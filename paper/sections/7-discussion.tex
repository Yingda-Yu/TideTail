\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Flow Matching produces plausible samples}
\label{subsec:why-fm}

Flow Matching is the only generator that passes the fidelity gate in every
wave of our programme — including the seven-family pilot of
Section~\ref{sec:results-benchmark-pilot}, where its two variants and WGAN-GP
are the only \textsc{inconclusive} (not \textsc{rejected}) generators
under the hard scale window $[0.5, 2.0]$ (WGAN-GP sits at 0.831–0.959, i.e.\
mildly under-dispersed, and the pilot is a fixed-budget comparison, so this is
not a family verdict). Three structural
differences explain Flow Matching's robustness, and each is a mechanism
rather than an empirical coincidence.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{One regression, one ODE.} The training objective is a single
    velocity-regression loss on straight-line interpolants between a Gaussian
    prior and the data. Inference integrates the learned field forward with a
    fixed-step Euler solver. There is no chain of 64 conditional updates whose
    errors compound, so the failure mode that dominates DDPM --- amplification of
    terminal-step epsilon error --- simply does not exist.
    \item \textbf{No small-variance sub-space.} Diffusion-TS first decomposes the
    signal into trend, seasonal and residual components and diffuses the
    residual, which concentrates the problem into a low-variance sub-space where
    a fixed absolute epsilon error is relatively enormous. Flow Matching diffuses
    nothing: it transports the full conditional residual distribution, so the
    signal-to-noise ratio of the regression target stays constant across
    training.
    \item \textbf{Conditioning enters as a feature, not as an additive bias.} The
    velocity network is a temporal convolutional network that consumes the
    history (and, in our physics variants, causal ERA5 summaries) directly, so
    conditioning cannot be lost through a rank deficiency such as the scalar
    latent broadcast that crippled the VAE.
\end{enumerate}

This does not make Flow Matching \emph{sufficient}: every FM run is formally
inconclusive because peak-tail agreement is not established at ten runs. It
makes it the only generator that is currently \emph{eligible} for a downstream
test.

\subsection{Why the other three implementations struggled}
\label{subsec:why-others}

None of the three failures is a property of its model family.

\paragraph{Conditional VAE: a sampling-path mismatch.}
The original defect (a scalar latent projection) was real and worth fixing, but
fixing it made sampling \emph{worse}. The reason is that the VAE samples from
$\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ while training fits a conditional posterior whose aggregate
has collapsed to a narrow distribution (0/32 active dimensions). The decoder is
therefore always evaluated slightly off-manifold, and a stronger
$z\mapsto$horizon path amplifies the discrepancy. The correct remedy is to make
training and sampling share a distribution --- free-bits KL, latent dropout, or a
learned conditional prior --- none of which we ran. Posterior collapse worsened
with training length (16/32 active dimensions at 4,000 windows versus 0/32 at
20,000), which is the standard dynamic and not a dataset property.

\paragraph{TimeGrad: an unstable autoregressive state.}
Consuming the ignored memory was necessary and helped (scale 10.55 $\rightarrow$
6.09), but the memory definition itself is unstable. A raw cumulative sum grows
monotonically across 72 horizon steps and was never seen during teacher-forced
training, so it diverges ($1.9\times10^{7}$ in the smaller pilot). A mean
aggregation removes the growth but also removes the signal, and the recursion
diverges in the opposite direction ($1.22\times10^{5}$). A bounded or decayed
memory is the obvious next hypothesis; we did not test it.

\paragraph{Diffusion-TS: numerical amplification in the objective.}
The original Fourier defect (noise spectrum versus clean spectrum) was real. The
repair replaced it with the implied clean signal, which requires dividing by
$\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$. At the terminal step this factor is $\approx0.024$, so
both the value and its gradient are amplified roughly $41\times$; the run reaches
NaN. The lesson is that an objective which is algebraically correct can still be
numerically unusable at a schedule's endpoint, and that a 2$\times$ improvement
observed at a small budget can reverse once the model actually fits the data.

\subsection{Why GRU and TCN respond differently}
\label{subsec:why-arch}

The paired interaction test (Section~\ref{sec:results-expanded}) bounds what
may be claimed: of ten TCN-minus-GRU contrasts, only three survive Bonferroni
correction --- US Gulf (standard and tail-aware arms, $-2.09$/$-2.06$~mm,
$p_{\text{bonf}} = 0.001$) and Southeast Australia (standard arm, $-1.29$~mm,
$p_{\text{bonf}} = 0.004$) --- and the Southeast Australia tail-aware arm
falls just short ($-1.22$~mm, $p_{\text{bonf}} = 0.008$). Eight of ten
interactions are negative (TCN benefits more or suffers less), but in the two
harm-dominated regions the direction either reverses (Charleston: TCN harmed
more, $+0.76$~mm, ns after correction) or is drowned by event variance
(Thames: $-2.41$~mm, CI $[-5.92,+0.83]$~mm).

We can state the comparison and bound the explanation, but we cannot prove
the mechanism. The most plausible account is capacity allocation: the TCN's
dilated receptive field integrates the full 96\,h history through stacked
convolutions, and synthetic windows add high-frequency, storm-like
variability that the convolutional filters can exploit as data-driven
regularisation. The GRU's gating saturates on the same inputs, so extra
samples shift the operating point rather than the effective receptive field.
An alternative account is that the GRU is already closer to its achievable
error floor on these splits, leaving less headroom for augmentation to
recover. Both accounts predict the observed direction in beneficial regions;
discriminating between them requires a receptive-field ablation and a
learning-curve study that we did not run, and we therefore present them as
hypotheses rather than findings.

\subsection{Why some regions benefit and others do not}
\label{subsec:why-region}

The five-region result is not a scatter around zero; it is structured, and
the structure is statistically confirmed. A Cochran--Q test rejects effect
homogeneity across regions for every augmentation arm
($p \le 1.4\times10^{-10}$; Holm-corrected pairwise contrasts localise the
differences), so region identity is a tested moderator of the augmentation
effect, not a descriptive pattern
(Section~\ref{sec:results-heterogeneity}).

The physical reading of the extremes is straightforward but not
established. Thames/North Sea, where all four augmentation cells harm
($+3.44$ to $+4.31\%$), has the largest tidal range in the set ($\pm3.3$~m
from FES2022, i.e.\ a 6.6~m range), and surge is estimated to account for
only 20--30\% of total water-level variance. A generator fitted to that
residual therefore produces samples dominated by noise, and appending them
adds variance without adding storm information. Thames also has 24.7\% NaN
gaps in its source series, which further weakens the fit. Southeast
Australia, where all four cells improve ($-1.29$ to $-3.02\%$), is
microtidal with a short, clean residual record. A single cross-region
correlation (tidal range versus effect, $N=5$) cannot separate tidal regime
from storm climatology, series length or data quality, and we do not make
that claim.

\subsection{Severity dependence: the effect attenuates where it matters most}
\label{subsec:why-severity}

Per-event severity analysis sharpens the regional picture
(Section~\ref{sec:results-heterogeneity}): in every region where a severity
gradient exists, augmentation effects --- beneficial and harmful alike ---
are concentrated in low-to-moderate severity events and attenuate at high
severity. Southeast Australia's benefit shrinks as event severity rises
(Spearman $\rho = +0.60$ to $+0.74$ across arms; low-severity median split
$-2.14\%$ versus $-0.42\%$ high-severity), and Thames' and Charleston's
harm shrinks correspondingly ($\rho = -0.34$ to $-0.68$; Thames median split
$+10.09\%$ versus $+1.81\%$). Gulf Coast and NY Bight show no severity
gradient, and no seasonal moderation survives correction (Holm-adjusted
$p > 0.15$ everywhere; a null we report rather than bury).

This is the single most operationally relevant finding of the programme:
generative augmentation, as implemented here, mostly informs the forecaster
about the \emph{frequent} regime's structure rather than the \emph{rare}
regime's dynamics. It directly motivates the bounded tail-calibration study
(Section~\ref{sec:results-tail-calibration}) --- and its negative prospect:
if the effect attenuates exactly where augmentation was supposed to help,
the burden of proof for tail-targeted generator variants is high.

\subsection{What independent-event evaluation changes}
\label{subsec:why-events}

Three things change relative to window-level evaluation, and all three move
in the conservative direction.

First, \textbf{the effective sample size collapses}. The five regional test
sets supply 20--51 event units each, not thousands of windows, so the
standard error of an extreme-event MAE is an order of magnitude larger than
a window-level analysis implies; the exact-enumeration physics contrasts
(n = 14--15) have a resolution floor of $1/16{,}384$ and the Monte-Carlo
floor of the larger contrasts is $9.999\times10^{-5}$ --- adequate for our
thresholds ($\ge 0.0018$), but any design promising finer resolution needs
more events, not more windows. Second, \textbf{multiplicity becomes
binding}: each pre-specified family carries its own correction
(Section~\ref{subsec:families}), and no result is reported from an
undeclared family. Third, \textbf{units are not storms}: the 180 regional
event units are not 180 independent storms --- declustering is
within-region, and synoptic drivers can couple nearby regions --- so every
p-value is a within-region paired statement, and regional heterogeneity is
tested on region-level estimates, never on pooled units.

\subsection{Global fidelity does not predict forecasting utility}
\label{subsec:why-fidelity-utility}

The programme's cleanest lesson is a disconnection between the two
qualities the augmentation literature usually optimises. The stratified
fidelity benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:results-generators}) scores every
generator on dispersion, Wasserstein distance, spectral and autocorrelation
structure, peak statistics and sample diversity across season and severity
strata. Yet the downstream five-region result was produced by Flow Matching
arms whose own fidelity status is \textsc{inconclusive} (peak-tail
agreement not established at the benchmark's sample sizes), while the
pilot's fidelity-\textsc{rejected} generators produced the only surviving
downstream effects --- all of them harms. Generation quality is neither
necessary nor sufficient for forecasting utility: a generator can be
distributionally excellent and operationally useless (Thames), and
distributionally mediocre and regionally useful (Southeast Australia). What
matters downstream is what the synthetic windows teach the forecaster about
the regimes it errs on --- which our severity analysis locates in the
low-to-moderate range, not the tail.

\subsection{Do physical priors help?}
\label{subsec:why-physics}

The completed six-arm ablation (Section~\ref{sec:results-physics-ablation})
replaces the earlier ``pending'' statement with a bounded answer. No arm
beats standard FM consistently: arm-versus-baseline deltas flip sign across
architectures and regions, and none survives correction. The only
Bonferroni-surviving contrasts are three Charleston GRU regulariser
contrasts (C2--C4), each indicating \emph{increased} error of
$0.55$--$0.57$~mm --- sub-millimetre in absolute terms, but unambiguous in
direction and seed-consistent. ERA5 conditioning alone is not reliably
beneficial either (C1 flips sign across seeds in NY Bight). The priors,
as implemented, are physics-inspired statistical regularisations fitted
train-only; they do not encode governing equations, and the reanalysis cell
($\approx$28\,km) versus gauge anchor ($4.5$--$11.7$~km) mismatch bounds
what any such regulariser can express. The honest conclusion is that, in
this design, adding physical structure to the generator neither produced a
benefit nor a mechanism-level signature beyond its own regularisation
penalty.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Retrospective test.} The 2022--2024 test years partially
    informed earlier exploratory waves (split design, threshold conventions);
    the primary result is a retrospective test, not an untouched confirmatory
    holdout. A genuinely confirmatory evaluation would require data beyond
    2024.
    \item \textbf{Generator-seed coverage.} Two generator seeds
    ($\{42, 99\}$) support a per-seed direction-consistency check, not a
    variance estimate; seed spread is reported per comparison but cannot be
    pooled into a confidence interval.
    \item \textbf{Baseline seed variance.} Identical A0 recipes differ by up
    to 25\% across downstream seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:seedvar}); per-unit
    seed-averaging controls the paired test, but absolute effect magnitudes
    inherit this spread.
    \item \textbf{Extreme-stratum power.} The fidelity benchmark's extreme
    stratum contains 4--5 units per region: all extreme-stratum fidelity
    results are \textsc{inconclusive} by design and never pooled into a tail
    claim.
    \item \textbf{Resolution.} ERA5 is 0.25$^\circ$; the Charleston anchor is
    11.7~km from its cell centre, so near-coastal pressure and wind are cell
    averages. No downscaling was attempted.
    \item \textbf{Causality.} Physics covariates are causal (history only), but
    the forcing is reanalysis. This is a reanalysis-conditioned experiment, not
    an operational forecast experiment.
    \item \textbf{Event units.} The expanded 2022--2024 units are POT-only, since
    the canonical-TC overlay is absent from the existing prediction archives.
    \item \textbf{Checkpoint reuse.} No existing checkpoint can be evaluated on
    2022--2024; the expanded protocol required retraining from scratch.
    \item \textbf{Compute.} Most recovery and benchmark work is CPU-only
    (GPU0 prohibited; heavy jobs strictly serial after a recorded
    systemd-oomd kill during a three-way concurrency incident); the
    five-region recompute used a single shared GPU. Conclusions are
    correspondingly scoped.
    \item \textbf{Thames data quality.} 24.7\% of the source timeline is NaN,
    which both reduces its unit count and weakens any generator fitted to it.
    \item \textbf{Pilot budgets.} The seven-family comparison gives every
    family ten epochs, fixed defaults and zero hyperparameter search; equal
    epochs are neither equal compute nor evidence of convergence, so its
    rejections are implementation-at-budget statements, not family verdicts.
    Its fidelity statistics are additionally computed on the first 200
    consecutive test windows (a single early-winter slice); the
    season-stratified revised evaluation
    (Section~\ref{sec:results-generators}) supersedes it for fidelity
    statements and the pilot artifact is preserved unchanged.
    \item \textbf{Tail calibration is exploratory.} The bounded
    tail-calibration study (Section~\ref{sec:results-tail-calibration}) uses
    the same retrospective test years; it is classified as an exploratory
    extension and does not carry confirmatory weight.
\end{itemize}
