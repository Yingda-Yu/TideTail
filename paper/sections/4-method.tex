\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Pipeline}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the comparison the paper is built around. A
conditional generator consumes the 96~h history of the non-tidal residual; in
the physics arms it additionally consumes causal reanalysis summaries of the
same 96~h window. Synthetic residuals are then appended to the real training
set, and a GRU or TCN forecaster is trained on a budget that is held fixed
across arms. The real-only arm (A0) uses the identical budget, split and
architecture, so the difference isolates the augmentation.

96~h preceding the forecast origin; the 72~h target horizon is never read.}

\subsection{Generators}
\label{subsec:generators}
Seven conditional generator families are compared, all consuming the 96\,h
history of the non-tidal residual as conditioning:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Flow Matching (standard)}. A velocity-field network
    (27{,}937 parameters) regresses the conditional velocity between a
    Gaussian prior and the data distribution on straight-line interpolants
    \citep{lipman2023flow}; samples are obtained by integrating the learned
    field with a fixed-step Euler solver.
    \item \textbf{Tail-aware Flow Matching}. The same architecture with a
    re-weighted objective that emphasises high-magnitude target windows
    (27{,}937 parameters).
    \item \textbf{Conditional VAE} (63{,}273 parameters encoder+decoder)
    \citep{kingma2014autoencoding} with a latent sampled from $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$.
    \item \textbf{TimeGrad}. An autoregressive DDPM rolled out over the
    horizon; the denoiser has 7{,}177 parameters \citep{rasul2021timegrad}.
    \item \textbf{Conditional DDPM}. A single-shot denoising model with a
    TemporalDenoiser backbone (27{,}937 parameters) \citep{ho2020denoising}.
    \item \textbf{Conditional WGAN-GP}. A generator (30{,}401 parameters) with
    a gradient-penalty critic (25{,}185 parameters; 55{,}586 total, always
    reported separately) \citep{gulrajani2017wgangp}.
    \item \textbf{Diffusion-TS}. Trend/seasonal/residual decomposition with
    the residual diffused by a ResidualDenoiser (39{,}425 parameters)
    \citep{yuan2024diffusionts}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Fairness contract.} Every family is trained with the same fixed
defaults --- ten epochs, batch size 256, identical optimiser settings, two
generator seeds (42, 99), zero hyperparameter search, and no model selection
in the benchmark. The benchmark therefore measures \emph{implementations at a
fixed budget}, not families at their best; parameter counts and realised
optimiser steps are disclosed per cell, and generator-only versus auxiliary
(e.g.\ critic) parameters are reported separately.

\subsection{Stratified fidelity evaluation}
\label{subsec:stratified-fidelity}
The pilot's fidelity scoring (first 200 consecutive test windows --- one
early-winter slice) is replaced by season-stratified scoring: windows are
assigned to local meteorological seasons (per hemisphere), and three strata
are drawn independently --- \emph{background} (below-median target peak,
40 per season), \emph{moderate tail} (q90 target peak, $\ge$120\,h
declustering) and \emph{extreme} (q95 target peak, $\ge$168\,h declustering,
POT scale). Every stratified cell is scored on the full metric panel:
scale/range ratios, Wasserstein-1, mean bias, q95/q99 exceedance ratios,
autocorrelation RMSE, power-spectral deviation, peak KS test, peak timing
and duration, sample diversity, exact and near-duplicate ratios, and
nearest-neighbour distance to the training set. Cells with fewer than two
independent windows are labelled \textsc{not-evaluated}; the extreme stratum
contains only 4--5 units per region, so \emph{all} extreme-stratum results
are reported as \textsc{inconclusive} by design and are never pooled into a
tail-quality claim.

\subsection{Physics-prior variants and what they are not}
\label{subsec:physics-priors}
The six-arm ablation (Section~\ref{sec:results-physics-ablation}) compares,
against standard FM: (i) ERA5 meteorological conditioning --- causal 96\,h
summaries of reanalysis pressure and wind at the gauge's reanalysis cell;
(ii) a pressure prior; (iii) a wind prior; (iv) the combined prior;
(v) a combined tail-aware arm; all as Flow Matching variants trained under
the same budget. Each prior enters as a physics-inspired \emph{statistical
regularisation}: a regression penalty that ties the generator's internal
delta to a fitted linear relation between real residuals and reanalysis
covariates. We stress the distinction in scope: ERA5 \emph{conditioning}
supplies covariate information; the \emph{regularisation} shapes the
generated distribution through a penalty; neither enforces a
\emph{governing-equation constraint}. The method is not a shallow-water or
other PDE-constrained generator, and no claim of that kind is made.

\subsection{Historical pilot DDPM architecture}
\label{subsec:pilot-ddpm}
The Wave 2.0 pilot used a 1D temporal DDPM with the following components:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal embedding}: sinusoidal frequencies $f_j = 10000^{-2j/D}$
    embedded into a width-\texttt{width} MLP, where $D$ is half the width.

    \item \textbf{History conditioning}: a separate MLP consumes all 96 past
    observations (72 in the pilot), producing a \texttt{width}-dimensional
    conditioning vector that is \emph{added} to the temporal embedding before
    entering the denoiser.

    \item \textbf{Dilated residual blocks}: four blocks with dilation $\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$,
    each containing GroupNorm (groups=4), SiLU activation, 3-conv padding to
    preserve length, and a linear condition injection.

    \item \textbf{Output head}: GroupNorm + SiLU + 3-conv producing a single-channel
    epsilon prediction at each of the 24 (pilot) or 72 (main-task) forecast horizons.
\end{enumerate}

Total parameter count: 27,169 (pilot 72$\rightarrow$24). For the main task 96$\rightarrow$72,
the horizon changes to 72 and the conditioning input to 96 values; the
benchmark implementation (Section~\ref{sec:results-benchmark-pilot}) counts
27{,}937 parameters, of the same order as the GRU and TCN budgets below
(exact counts per family are reported with the pilot results).

\subsection{Noise schedule and forward process}
We implement cosine cumulative noise schedule~\citep{ho2020denoising} with
$T=64$ diffusion steps in the pilot:
\begin{equation}
    \beta_t = 1 - \frac{\cos((t+0.008)/1.008 \cdot \pi/2)^2}{\cos((t-1+0.008)/1.008 \cdot \pi/2)^2},
    \qquad \alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t, \qquad \bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{i=1}^{t} \alpha_i.
\end{equation}
At $t=T=64$, the historical schedule reaches $\beta_{64} \approx 0.999$ and
$\bar{\alpha}_{64} \approx 5.93 \times 10^{-7}$, producing $\approx 1299\times$
clean amplification at the final reverse step. An oracle comparison (Section~\ref{sec:results})
shows that subsampling a $T'=1000$ cosine schedule to 64 steps reaches
$\bar{\alpha}_{64} \approx 5.89 \times 10^{-4}$ (41$\times$ amplification),
which eliminates the catastrophic scale explosion.

\subsection{Training objective}
Training targets \emph{unit Gaussian epsilon} at each timestep. The loss is mean
squared error between the network's epsilon prediction and the actual injected
Gaussian noise:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)} \left[
        \left\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t} \epsilon, t, \text{history}) \right\|^2
    \right].
\end{equation}
Training hyperparameters for the pilot run: AdamW with $\text{lr}=0.001$,
batch size 128, three epochs, BF16 precision on a single RTX3090 GPU, seed
20261025.

\subsection{Forecasting baselines}
All baselines consume 96 hours of hourly history and produce 72 hours of
forecast, using the same train-only normalization as the diffusion model.
Missing-value masks are concatenated as an extra input channel.

\paragraph{Rule-based baselines.}
\emph{Persistence} carries forward the last observed water level for all 72 hours.
\emph{Seasonal baseline} repeats the most recent semi-diurnal tidal cycle
(144 hours $\approx$ 6 days) to capture the dominant tidal periodicity.

\paragraph{GRU encoder-decoder.}
A 2-layer GRU encoder maps the 96-hour history into a hidden state; a GRU
decoder then autoregressively produces 72 forecast steps, each step feeding
its own output back as input. Hidden size is chosen to keep the
total parameter count of the same order as the DDPM's ($\approx$ 5--10K);
the exact per-family counts are reported with the pilot results. The GRU operates on normalized values with explicit
gradient tracking for training and a no-grad \texttt{predict\_eval} path for
inference.

\paragraph{Temporal Convolutional Network.}
A stack of dilated causal 1D convolutions with Chomp1d padding ensures causality.
Dilation grows exponentially ($\{1, 2, 4, 8\}$) to achieve a receptive field
covering the full 96-hour history. The final hidden-state channel at the
latest timestep is projected to all 72 horizons \emph{at once} (non-autoregressive).
Channel count is tuned to match DDPM budget ($\approx$ 15--20K).

\subsection{Training-set augmentation}
\emph{All augmentations modify the training set only.} Validation and test data
are never augmented. Three augmentation strategies are implemented:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A1 — Balanced extreme-event oversampling.} Windows where any target
    cell exceeds the train-only q95 threshold receive a higher sampling weight
    ($\approx$ 3$\times$) in each optimizer step. This addresses the rare-event
    training imbalance without fabricating new observations. Implemented via
    \texttt{torch.utils.data.WeightedRandomSampler}.

    \item \textbf{A2 — Residual noise perturbation.} Training history (not target)
    is perturbed with additive Gaussian noise whose standard deviation is 30\% of
    the train-only residual standard deviation. This models instrument uncertainty
    and sub-hourly unresolved variability. Targets are \emph{never} modified.

    \item \textbf{A2 — Tidal phase shifting.} Both history and target windows are
    circular-shifted by a random fraction of the semi-diurnal tidal cycle
    (12-hour period, maximum shift 6 hours). This exploits tidal phase symmetry
    to create valid phase-shifted training pairs while preserving the residual
    forecast relationship.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Engineering validation guards}
After the pilot revealed catastrophic scale explosion, we added validation guards
that \emph{reject} non-finite or implausibly scaled samples. These are engineering
checks, not model repairs:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Nonfinite rejection}: raise on nonfinite conditioning history,
    nonfinite epsilon prediction at any reverse step, or nonfinite reverse sample
    intermediate state.

    \item \textbf{Train-reference scale screen}: after de-normalization, compare
    sample standard deviation to frozen training target standard deviation. Reject
    if ratio $< 0.1$ (collapse) or $> 5.0$ (dispersion), or if mean offset exceeds
    5 training standard deviations. Both bounds are explicit policy defaults, not
    estimates of physical plausibility.
\end{enumerate}

The historical GPU pilot predates these guards; its samples have SD ratio 22.95
and would be rejected by the screen.
