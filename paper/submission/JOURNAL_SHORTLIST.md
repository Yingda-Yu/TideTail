## Shortlist (in recommended order)

### 1. Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences (NHESS) — Copernicus
- Scope fit: extreme coastal water levels, storm surge, hazard forecasting,
  and machine-learning methodology are all squarely in scope; the journal
  explicitly welcomes rigorous negative/null results and reproducibility
  scrutiny — a strong match for our region-dependent augmentation effects
  and honest INCONCLUSIVE framing.
- Article type: Research article.
- Machine-learning contribution: accepted as core methodology, not a gimmick.
- Reference style: Copernicus (author-year), native LaTeX template available.
- APC/policies: open access with APC (to verify current amount); Copernicus
  allows preprint-first workflows, which matches our GitHub preprint state.
- Risk: broad readership; the fully computational framing must be written
  hazard-first (our title/abstract already do this).

### 2. Coastal Engineering — Elsevier
- Scope fit: coastal water-level forecasting, tide--surge interaction,
  gauge-based prediction studies are standard fare; recent volumes include
  many neural/surrogate-model papers.
- Article type: Research paper.
- Machine-learning contribution: accepted; the seven-family generative
  benchmark is beyond the journal's usual ML depth, which is a plus.
- Reference style: Elsevier numbered or author-year (to verify per current
  guide for authors).
- Policies: subscription or hybrid OA (APC only if OA chosen, to verify).
- Risk: engineering applications bias; the statistical-audit material may
  need compression in the cover letter framing.

### 3. Environmental Modelling & Software — Elsevier
- Scope fit: methodological rigour in environmental modelling; reproducible
  workflows, evaluation protocols and model-comparison studies are the
  journal's core identity — matches our protocol-frozen, artifact-traceable
  design and the fidelity-vs-utility gap as a generalisable lesson.
- Article type: Research paper / technical note (research paper recommended).
- Machine-learning contribution: central; generative time-series methods
  appear regularly.
- Reference style: Elsevier author-year.
- Policies: hybrid OA (to verify).
- Risk: less coastal-hazards readership; surge-specific reviewers may be
  rarer.

## Rejected/deferred options (for the record)
- *Nature Communications / Communications Earth & Environment*: single-claim
  breadth expected; our deliberately bounded, mostly-negative multi-part
  result fits worse and review risk is high.
- *Ocean Modelling*: strong fit for surge dynamics, but our contribution is
  data-driven augmentation, not a dynamical model.
- *Frontiers in Marine Science*: scope fits but APC and review-perception
  concerns for the target authorship; kept as backup only.
