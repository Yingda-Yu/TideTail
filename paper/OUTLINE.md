# TideTail — paper outline (Wave 3.2; results consolidated, manuscript in completion)

Working title: **When Does Generative Augmentation Help Extreme Coastal
Water-Level Forecasting? Architecture Dependence, Physical Priors, and
Independent-Event Validation**

1. Introduction: extreme-event scarcity; H1 stated in original form; why the
   question is harder than it looks (window dependence, census
   double-counting, implementation-vs-family attribution, global averaging);
   seven contributions; no universal H1 claim; retrospective-test disclosure.
2. Related work: coastal water-level and storm-surge forecasting; extreme-value
   statistics; time-series augmentation; conditional GANs/VAEs; diffusion
   time-series models; Flow Matching; physics-guided ML; event-level
   evaluation; reliability of generative evaluation. All references verified
   against primary sources.
3. Data and problem formulation: five admitted regions; non-tidal residual
   target (`water_level_m` already residual, never de-tided twice); frozen
   96h→72h contract; leakage gates (event-group disjointness, chronological
   order, full-support embargo); frozen primary protocol
   2015–2020/2021/2022–2024; retest disclosure.
4. Method: seven generator families under one fairness contract (ten epochs,
   batch 256, seeds {42,99}, zero search); stratified fidelity evaluation
   (season × background/moderate/extreme strata; extreme n=4–5 INCONCLUSIVE
   by design); GRU/TCN forecasters; six physics-prior arms distinguished as
   conditioning vs physics-inspired statistical regularisation, explicitly
   NOT PDE constraints.
5. Experimental design: comparison groups (A0/A3/A4 × GRU/TCN); declared
   multiple-testing families; seeds and reproducibility metadata; checkpoint
   verification (SHA256, param counts); hardware honesty (GPU0 prohibited,
   serial heavy jobs).
6. Results: census (679, not 707); four-generator production benchmark +
   recovery study (implementation defects, not family verdicts); seven-family
   pilot (preserved historical); corrected-event-level analysis (320-test
   family preserved as historical); five-region primary result (12/20
   Bonferroni survivors, 6 beneficial / 6 harmful); architecture interaction
   (3/10 corrected); regional heterogeneity (Cochran-Q p≤1.4e-10), severity
   attenuation, season null; six-arm physics ablation (3/20 survive, all
   Charleston GRU harm); stratified seven-family fidelity (70 cells); bounded
   tail-calibration extension (exploratory).
7. Discussion: why FM is robust; why other implementations failed; why the
   architecture interaction is bounded; why regions differ; severity
   dependence; what event-level evaluation changes; fidelity ≠ utility;
   completed physics answer; limitations.
8. Conclusion: the dependence map; what is established; negatives preserved;
   what a confirmatory answer would require.
